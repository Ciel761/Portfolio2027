Sense and Senseability: Exploring Future Immersive Environments for Scholarly Sensemaking

2025

Creativity & Cognition 2025 · Pictorial · Siyi Zhu and Joel Chan

Scholars work with questions, concepts, assertions, evidence, and theories whose important qualities are usually discovered through effortful reading and reasoning. This project asks how immersive environments might make that abstract information more “senseable.” A systematic review of scholarly sensemaking systems and an analysis of visual strategies in modern art produced a design toolkit and three conceptual environments for embodied scholarly work.

From Information to Form

Systematic review
949 ACM Digital Library records were screened to 83 studies of scholarly sensemaking and then to 38 studies focused on abstract, qualitative scholarly information.

Art exploration
Three modern art museums yielded observations of 113 artworks and 365 photographs, which were synthesized into reusable visual strategies.

Concept generation
Information properties and visual strategies were combined like design cards to generate immersive sensemaking concepts.

A Design Toolkit for Senseability

The review distinguishes materialized information objects - including documents, citations, data, summaries, notes, reviews, and concepts - from immaterial objects such as research directions, contributions, assertions, theories, and evidence. The distinction exposes how much scholarly work remains trapped in text or informal mental models.

Properties
Position, accessibility, quantity, connections, similarity, context, evocativeness, structure, timeliness, quality, fairness, explicitness, truthlikeness, and completeness.

Visual patterns
Space, repetition, composition, texture, fidelity, and cross-section offer ways to translate intangible properties into perceivable spatial behavior.

The toolkit does not prescribe a single interface. It gives designers a vocabulary for pairing an information property with a visual behavior and asking how that behavior could support embodied reasoning.

City of Information

Assertions, evidence, and questions become typed building blocks in a persistent virtual city. Placement rules make semantic relationships tangible: for example, a claim can sit on evidence that supports it.

Building height conveys evocativeness, proximity signals relevance, and repeated structures reveal patterns across a growing body of knowledge.

As the city expands, landmarks support retrieval and undeveloped regions suggest possible research directions.

Rusted Text

Rusted Text uses texture and material as a “digital patina” for timeliness. New information begins as paper; frequently revisited objects become thicker and more permanent.

Neglected objects wrinkle, stiffen, rust, crack, and eventually break into archived debris. Age and attention become qualities that scholars can perceive at a glance.

The concept replaces an abstract timestamp with an embodied material history of a scholar’s relationship to information.

Unshaped Matter

A shifting, gas-like form represents the incomplete and implicit state of an emerging research idea. Mirrors around it surface papers and notes from different perspectives.

Speaking about the idea records new notes and retrieves related information. As the idea becomes more explicit and complete, the matter shrinks and takes on a clearer form.

Moving around the object changes the angle of reflection, turning bodily position into a way to reveal different connections and possible interpretations.
